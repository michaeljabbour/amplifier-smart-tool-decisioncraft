# Vision (draft)

Most decisions that matter affect several groups at once: the people who pay, the people
who do the work, the people who use the result, and the people who keep it safe. They are
usually made in a meeting, from slides, by whoever speaks most.

Decisioncraft makes the decision itself the thing people look at. One map shows how the
situation works today and what is planned. The evidence sits beside each claim. Every
point of view gets written down and ends in a question, so the review produces answers,
not just discussion.

## What it should feel like

- **Anyone can read it.** A customer, a nurse, a council officer or a finance lead can open
  the file and follow it without being shown how.
- **The reasons stay attached.** Months later, a decision still shows what it was based on:
  the quote, the figure, the source and the date.
- **Every voice is visible.** Roles are explicit, so it is clear when one is missing.
- **It ends in a decision.** Questions are ranked; decisions have owners and dates.
- **It keeps learning.** Outcome measures feed back as evidence for the next decision.

## Where agents fit

An agent can draft a model from notes and data, add notes from each role, check links and
freshness, and compare versions. People read, answer and decide. The tool keeps that line
clear: model-backed steps are named as such and their output is always checked.

## Not the goal

- Live collaborative editing. Offline reviews travel as files. A local review started
  by an agent can save answers directly on the same computer.
- Project tracking after the decision.
- Giving advice in specialist fields. The medical example shows team-level organisation,
  not clinical guidance.

## A review should feel like a conversation

Begin with the decision and enough context to understand it. Ask one useful question at
a time. Show a clear recommendation without treating it as the answer. Let people
choose when to compare their view with other people's responses.
Keep sources close enough to check, without making people read a pile of documents first.

People can say they are not sure, leave a question for later, and change their answer.
Before they finish, show what they said and what remains open. Make saving and the next
step visible. A vote shows what deserves attention; it does not make a decision.
