# Three Oaks Bakery: a monthly box?

**The decision:** Should our three bakeries offer a monthly subscription box, and if so, what shape should it take?

Three Oaks is a small chain of three bakeries. Regulars have asked for a box they can collect or have delivered each month. This map shows what a subscriber would go through, where the idea comes from, and what is still open. All names and numbers are made up.

_Checked against the sources on 2026-03-28. Checked against six customer chats, a year of sales and cost figures, and one staff meeting._

## Gaps between today and planned

### G1: Let people pause a month (impact 5/5, effort 2/5)
The first thing a regular asked for. Without it, people avoid signing up.

- As a subscriber going on holiday, I want to skip next month by replying to a text, so I am not charged for bread I cannot collect.
  - Done when: a reply of SKIP before Wednesday stops that month's charge
  - Done when: the shop's packing list for Saturday leaves that person out
  - Done when: the subscriber gets a text confirming the skip
- **Analyst note (Should decide): Define the skip deadline.** Packing happens Thursday, so a skip on Friday is too late.
  We suggest: Skips close at the end of Wednesday.
  Question: Is Wednesday a fair deadline for skipping?
  > “I'd pay for a box if I could skip a month when we're away. I hate being locked in.” — Customer chats, March, Regular, Mill Lane [E1]

### G2: Say what is in the box, and offer a gluten-free swap (impact 4/5, effort 2/5)
People will not buy a surprise they might not eat.

- As a gluten-free regular, I want to choose a swap when I sign up, so every box has something I can eat.
  - Done when: the sign-up asks about dietary needs
  - Done when: the packing list shows each box's swaps
  - Done when: the counter card shows a sample box with prices

### G3: Someone owns failed payments (impact 3/5, effort 3/5)
Counter staff will not chase money, and nobody else is named.

- As the owner, I want failed payments sent to one named person each week, so boxes are not packed for people who have not paid.
  - Done when: a weekly list of failed payments goes to the named person
  - Done when: the box is held, not packed, until paid
- **Finance note (Should decide): Failed payments cost more than the fee.** A packed box nobody paid for is £14 lost.
  We suggest: Hold the box until the payment goes through.
  Question: Do we pack boxes for people whose payment failed, or hold them?
  > “A trial box costs £11.40 in ingredients and packaging, plus about 14 minutes of staff time to pack.” — Sales and costs, last 12 months, Costs section [E6]
  > “The counter team can take sign-ups but not chase failed payments.” — Staff meeting, Station Road, Mill Lane manager [E10]

### G4: Invoices for office buyers (impact 3/5, effort 4/5)
Office buyers cannot pay by personal card.

- As an office buyer, I want a monthly invoice, so I can claim it back.
  - Done when: an invoice is sent on the 1st with the company's name
  - Done when: unpaid invoices after 30 days pause the box
- **Owner note (For information): Invoices can wait.** Only one buyer asked, and invoices need more admin than the pilot can carry.
  We suggest: Do invoices after the pilot if office demand is real.
  Question: Do we say no to office buyers during the pilot?
  > “Friday treats for the team would be great, but I need an invoice, not a card charge.” — Customer chats, March, Office buyer, Station Road [E2]

## Words we use

- **churn**: The share of subscribers who cancel in a given month.
- **margin**: What is left from the price after ingredients, packaging, fees and staff time.
- **pause**: Skipping a month without cancelling.
