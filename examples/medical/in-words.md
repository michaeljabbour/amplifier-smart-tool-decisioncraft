# Going home well: changing how our ward discharges patients

**The decision:** How should our ward change the way it sends patients home, so fewer come back within 30 days?

A worked example about how a hospital ward organises its work. It is not medical advice, and every name, quote and number is invented. A 28-bed medical ward sees one in seven patients readmitted within a month, often because of confusion about medicines. This map follows a patient's way home and the ward's discharge day, and lists what the team needs to decide.

_Checked against the sources on 2026-08-14. Checked against a six-month ward audit, patient and carer feedback, and a staff huddle. All invented._

## Gaps between today and planned

### G1: Tomorrow's list, agreed the evening before (impact 5/5, effort 2/5)
Lets the pharmacy get medicines ready by noon instead of the evening.

- As the ward pharmacist, I want tomorrow's list by 6 pm, so medicines are ready by noon.
  - Done when: a list is agreed at the evening handover every weekday
  - Done when: at least 70% of listed patients leave before 2 pm
  - Done when: the list never sends anyone home before a doctor confirms in the morning

### G2: A one-page 'what changed' sheet (impact 5/5, effort 2/5)
Medicine confusion is behind about a third of readmissions on this ward.

- As a patient going home, I want one page showing new, changed and stopped medicines, so I take the right ones.
  - Done when: every discharge includes the sheet
  - Done when: the nurse goes through it with the patient or carer
  - Done when: patients can say back which medicines are new

### G3: A call within two days (impact 4/5, effort 3/5)
Patients did not know who to call when they felt worse.

- As a patient at home, I want a call within two days, so I can ask questions before small problems grow.
  - Done when: 90% of patients get a call within two working days
  - Done when: the call follows a short script and is recorded
- **Quality analyst note (Should decide): Watch for side effects of the change.** Calling every patient takes coordinator time from somewhere else.
  We suggest: Record what the calls find and how long they take.
  Question: What would we stop doing to make time for the calls?
  > “Follow-up phone calls happen when there is time, which is rarely.” — Ward staff huddle, Discharge coordinator [E11]
- **Finance note (Should decide): Calls cost time; readmissions cost more.** Each readmission uses a bed for several days. A short call takes about ten minutes.
  We suggest: Count coordinator hours during the trial to compare.
  Question: Can we fund extra coordinator hours for eight weeks?
  > “58 of 412 patients (14%) were readmitted within 30 days.” — Ward audit: readmissions within 30 days, Summary [E1]
  > “Follow-up phone calls happen when there is time, which is rarely.” — Ward staff huddle, Discharge coordinator [E11]

### G4: Checklist in the ward system (impact 3/5, effort 3/5)
Paper checklists are often half-filled.

- As a nurse, I want the checklist in the ward system, so nothing is missed and the next shift can see it.
  - Done when: a patient cannot be marked discharged with required items blank

## Words we use

- **discharge**: When a patient leaves hospital to go home or to another place of care.
- **readmission**: Coming back into hospital within a set time after leaving, here 30 days.
- **discharge letter**: The summary sent to the patient's family doctor about their stay and their medicines.
- **tomorrow's list**: The patients the team expects to send home the next day, agreed the evening before.

## Who is speaking

- **Patient experience**: Will patients and carers understand what happens and what to do?
- **Quality analyst**: What exactly are we measuring, and could the change make anything worse?
- **Ward pharmacist**: Can medicines be ready, checked and explained in time?
- **Ward manager**: What do we change first, who does it, and how do we know it worked?
- **Privacy and safety**: Who sees patient information, and what could go wrong?
- **Patient and carer voice**: What would make going home feel safe?

- **AI agent teammate**: What routine work could an assistant do, always checked by a clinician?
- **Finance**: What does it cost in staff time, and what does it save?
