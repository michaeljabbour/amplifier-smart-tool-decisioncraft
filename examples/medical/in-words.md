# Going home well: changing how our ward discharges patients

**The decision:** How should our ward change the way it sends patients home, so fewer come back within 30 days?

A worked example about how a hospital ward organises its work. It is not medical advice, and every name, quote and number is invented. A 28-bed medical ward sees one in seven patients readmitted within a month, often because of confusion about medicines. This map follows a patient's way home and the ward's discharge day, and lists what the team needs to decide.

**How to read this:** This is a worked example about how a ward organises its work; it is not medical advice and everything in it is invented. The first view follows a patient and their carer home: how it goes today, how it would go with the proposed changes, and what the carer goes through. The second view shows the ward's side: the evening list, getting medicines ready, discharge day itself, and the calls and letters afterwards. Each box says whether it works today, partly works, is missing, or is planned. Turn on Show technical names to see which systems, forms and roles are involved. Notes from the pharmacist, ward manager, quality analyst, patient voice and others sit on the boxes they are about.

_Checked against the sources on 2026-08-14. Checked against a six-month ward audit, patient and carer feedback, a staff huddle, two weeks of timing observations, calls with five family doctor practices, a language and access sample, and a two-week try-out of follow-up calls. All invented._

## Words we use

- **discharge**: When a patient leaves hospital to go home or to another place of care.
- **readmission**: Coming back into hospital within a set time after leaving, here 30 days.
- **discharge letter**: The summary sent to the patient's family doctor about their stay and their medicines.
- **tomorrow's list**: The patients the team expects to send home the next day, agreed the evening before.
- **ward round**: When the doctors see each patient on the ward, usually in the morning, and decide the plan for the day.
- **discharge lounge**: A room where patients who are ready to go can wait for transport or medicines, freeing their bed.
- **handover**: When one shift tells the next about every patient.
- **say back**: Asking the patient to explain in their own words, to check they understood.
