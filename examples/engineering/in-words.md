# Riverside footbridge: repair or replace?

**The decision:** Should the council repair the Riverside footbridge, replace it, or replace it with a wider bridge for walking and cycling?

A 1974 steel footbridge is corroding. It is the only step-free crossing to a school and a row of shops. This map shows the options, what a closure would be like for the people who use the bridge, and what the council still has to decide. All names and figures are made up.

_Checked against the sources on 2026-06-30. Checked against the principal inspection, early cost estimates and one public drop-in session._

## Gaps between today and planned

### G1: A way across during a long closure (impact 5/5, effort 4/5)
A nine-month closure without a crossing cuts off the school's only step-free route.

- As a parent with a pram, I want a step-free way across while the bridge is shut, so the school run stays under ten minutes.
  - Done when: a temporary crossing or shuttle runs at school times
  - Done when: the route is signed and lit
  - Done when: the detour is no more than 10 minutes longer

### G2: Ramps people can use on their own (impact 4/5, effort 2/5)
The north ramps are too steep for some wheelchair users.

- As a wheelchair user, I want ramps I can manage alone, so I do not need help to cross.
  - Done when: ramp slopes meet the access standard
  - Done when: a wheelchair user tests the design before it is approved

### G3: Bid to the cycling fund by November (impact 4/5, effort 3/5)
Up to 40% of a wider bridge could be paid for, but only with a bid in time.

- As the council lead, I want the bid submitted by November, so the wider option is affordable.
  - Done when: a bid is submitted before the deadline
  - Done when: the bid shows the drop-in feedback

### G4: Weekly news during works (impact 2/5, effort 1/5)
People put up with closures better when they can see progress.

- As a resident, I want a weekly update, so I know the end date is real.
  - Done when: a photo and a line of news go up every Friday

## Words we use

- **load rating**: The weight a bridge can safely carry, worked out by an engineer.
- **bearings**: The parts at each end that let the bridge move a little as it warms and cools.
- **section loss**: Steel lost to rust, as a share of the original thickness.
- **shared use**: A path for people walking and cycling together.

## Who is speaking

- **Access and design**: Can everyone use it, including people with prams, wheelchairs and bikes?
- **Analyst**: Are the costs and lives compared on the same basis?
- **Structural engineer**: Is it safe, how long will it last, and what could go wrong during the work?
- **Council lead**: What do we recommend to the committee, and what do we tell residents?
- **Safety**: How do we keep people safe before, during and after the work?
- **Resident voice**: What does this mean for the people who cross every day?
- **AI agent teammate**: What routine work could an assistant take on, with a person checking?
- **Finance**: What does each option cost over its whole life, and who pays?
