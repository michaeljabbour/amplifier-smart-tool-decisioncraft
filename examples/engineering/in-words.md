# Riverside footbridge: repair or replace?

**The decision:** Should the council repair the Riverside footbridge, replace it, or replace it with a wider bridge for walking and cycling?

A 1974 steel footbridge is corroding. It is the only step-free crossing to a school and a row of shops. This map shows the options, what a closure would be like for the people who use the bridge, and what the council still has to decide. All names and figures are made up.

**How to read this:** Three views. The first traces the decision from what the inspection found to a bridge people can use, and whether it worked; switch between Today and Planned to see what exists and what is proposed. The second, The works, option by option, follows each option from committee to reopening across the council, designers, contractor, river authority and funders, so you can compare how long each takes and who it depends on. The third shows what a resident goes through before, during and after a closure. Turn on Show technical names to see engineering detail. Notes from the structural engineer, access, safety, finance and residents sit on the boxes they are about.

_Checked against the sources on 2026-06-30. Checked against the principal inspection, early costs, two weeks of crossing counts, an access audit, quotes for temporary crossings, the programme and suppliers, a shops survey, and one public drop-in session._

## Words we use

- **load rating**: The weight a bridge can safely carry, worked out by an engineer.
- **bearings**: The parts at each end that let the bridge move a little as it warms and cools.
- **section loss**: Steel lost to rust, as a share of the original thickness.
- **shared use**: A path for people walking and cycling together.
- **rest landing**: A flat section partway up a ramp where people can stop.
- **fabricated steel**: Steel parts cut, shaped and welded in a factory before they come to site.
- **framework**: A list of contractors the council has already vetted and can hire without a full tender.
- **programme**: The timetable for the works, week by week.
