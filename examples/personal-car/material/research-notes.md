# Research notes: published figures used in this example

The people and documents in this example are fictional. These figures are real and
dated; check them again before relying on them.

- AAA, "Your Driving Costs" 2025 (September 2025): owning and running a new car costs
  $11,577 a year on average; depreciation is the largest cost at $4,334 a year; finance
  $1,131 a year; full-cover insurance $1,694 a year; maintenance, repair and tyres 11.04
  cents a mile; regular petrol averaged $3.151 a gallon over the study year.
  newsroom.aaa.com/2025/09/aaa-new-vehicle-costs-drop-to-11577/
- Kelley Blue Book (Cox Automotive), August 2026: the average new-vehicle transaction
  price was $50,089. coxautoinc.com/insights/august-2026-atp-report/
- Kelley Blue Book: cars lose about 20% of their value in the first year and about 60%
  over the first five years. kbb.com/car-advice/how-to-beat-car-depreciation/
- Edmunds: average APR on new-car loans 6.7% in Q4 2025; average price of a
  three-year-old used vehicle $31,216 in Q2 2025. edmunds.com
- U.S. Energy Information Administration: regular petrol averaged $3.10 a gallon in
  2025. eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=66784
- Leasing: a money factor times 2,400 gives the rough yearly rate (0.0025 is about 6%).
  Monthly payment = (price − residual) ÷ months + (price + residual) × money factor.
- Ownership costs rise with age: households with cars 6 to 15 years old paid more for
  repairs each year than those with newer cars (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics,
  "Americans' aging autos").
- Pre-mortem: imagine the decision has already gone badly and list why. Gary Klein,
  "Performing a Project Premortem", Harvard Business Review, September 2007; it builds
  on research finding that imagining an outcome has happened improves the ability to
  name reasons for it by about 30% (Mitchell, Russo and Pennington, 1989).
- 10-10-10: ask how you will feel about a choice in 10 minutes, 10 months and 10 years
  (Suzy Welch, 10-10-10, 2009).
- Weighting good practice: weights are judgments, so use few levels, show totals to one
  decimal, call results within a small margin a close call, and test which weight
  changes would change the winner (decision-matrix and swing-weight guidance, e.g.
  INCOSE "Using the Swing Weight Matrix to Weight Multiple Objectives").
