# Renew, keep or buy: Dana's next car

**The decision:** When the lease ends in March, should Dana's family keep and repair the car, renew the lease, buy new, buy used, or go car-free?

Car share is cheapest but fails the school run. Keeping the old car costs least over five years, unless repairs run high. Buying used is the sturdier choice: about $2,500 more than keeping, $11,000 less than leasing, with long warranty cover. The scores are a close call between buying new and buying used.

**How to read this:** Start with the list on the left. The scoring table checks the must-haves first, then weighs what matters; try your own weights with Change the weights. ## Words we use

- **real cost**: Money spent so far, plus any loan still owed, minus what the car could be sold for.
- **buyout**: Buying the car you have been leasing, at the price set when the lease began.
- **residual**: What the leasing company expects the car to be worth at the end of the lease.
- **certified**: A used car checked by the maker's dealer and sold with extra warranty cover.
- **must-have**: Something an option has to meet. If it fails, it is out, whatever its score.
- **what-if**: A change to one assumption, such as petrol prices, to see how the answer moves.
- **pre-mortem**: Imagining the decision has already gone badly and listing why, before deciding.

## Who is speaking

- **Money**: What does it really cost over the years, and what does the monthly number hide?
- **Practical expert**: What tends to go wrong with this choice, and what does it take to keep it working?
- **Safety**: Is anyone put at risk, and is the safest option out of reach for a good reason?
- **People affected**: Who else lives with this choice every day, and what do they need?
- **Future you**: Will this still suit you in a few years, and how easy is it to change course?
- **Environment**: What does each option use up, and is there a lighter way to get the same result?
- **Market and resale**: What is it worth later, and is now a good or bad time to buy or sell?
- **Devil's advocate**: What if the question itself is wrong? Is there an option nobody has named?
